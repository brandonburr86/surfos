wview - the Image Viewer: pictures in a window

SYNOPSIS
    wview [FILE]

DESCRIPTION
    A window that shows a BMP, PNG, GIF or JPEG picture, fitted to the
    window, at its actual size or zoomed from 1/64 to 16 times, and
    moved about when it is larger than the window. What kind a file is
    comes from its first bytes, not from its name. The picture's
    transparency shows over squares of grey.

    The desktop starts it from Files and from Apps > Image Viewer;
    "wview FILE" in the Terminal shows FILE. Open chooses a picture
    with the desktop's chooser. The next and previous picture are the
    other pictures of the file's folder, by name, numbers counted as
    numbers, around the ends. Details shows the file's name, folder,
    kind, size, the picture's size, when it was changed and its place
    in the folder. Copy path puts the file's whole path on the
    desktop's clipboard.

KEYS
    O              Open a picture
    N, Space       the folder's next picture
    P, Backspace   its previous picture
    F, 0           fit the picture to the window
    1              its actual size
    + and -        zoom in and out
    arrows         move a zoomed picture; Page Up, Page Down, Home
                   and End too, and the wheel, or drag it
    I              the details; Esc puts them away
    Ctrl+C         copy the path (the desktop's Edit > Copy)
    Esc, Ctrl+W    close the window

    A double-click switches between fitting and the actual size.

LIMITS
    A file is read whole: 32 MB at most, or 62 MB for a BMP, whose
    pixels are stored as they are (16 megapixels of 32 bits). A
    picture of 4096 pixels a side and 16 megapixels at most; a GIF's
    first frame only; JPEG baseline and progressive (32 scans at most),
    not arithmetic, 12-bit or lossless. A folder's first 4096 pictures
    are stepped through. Opening a file from Files shows it in the
    Image Viewer's one window, in place of the picture there; File >
    Open with starts another. It shows and never changes a picture:
    there is nothing to save.
