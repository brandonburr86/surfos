top - a live view of the tasks

SYNOPSIS
    top [-d seconds] [-n count] [-s cpu|mem|pid]

DESCRIPTION
    Shows every task and what it takes of the processor and of memory,
    with the machine's totals above, read again every two seconds. It is
    the kernel's list, so kernel threads are there too, their names in
    brackets.

    On a terminal this is a live view on the alternate screen, which
    works on a console, in the desktop's Terminal and over telnet. After
    count reports it stays for one more wait and gives the shell's
    screen back.

    With standard output anything else, top prints the same report as
    plain text again and again, a blank line between them, until count
    or until its reader goes.

    The first report's shares are since boot, the others' since the
    report before. The kernel counts a timer tick to whichever task it
    finds running, so %CPU is a sample at 100 ticks a second: over two
    seconds it is good to a tick or two, and a task that runs only
    between ticks shows nothing.

OPTIONS
    -d seconds  The wait between reports, 0.1 to 86400, in decimals if
                you like. 2 without it.
    -n count    End after that many reports. No end without it.
    -s order    The order to begin with: cpu, the most first and the
                default; mem, the same; or pid. In plain reports this is
                the only way to choose.

KEYS
    c, m, p    Order by processor, memory or pid.
    Up, Down   Move through a list longer than the screen. PgUp, PgDn,
               Home and End too.
    Space      Read again now.
    q, Ctrl+C  End.

COLUMNS
    S         The state: R running, r ready, B blocked, S sleeping, T
              stopped, Z ended and not collected yet.
    TTY       The console F1 to F4, a pseudo-terminal pty0 and on, or -
              for none.
    %CPU      Its share of the processor.
    TIME      All the processor time it has had, in
              hours:minutes:seconds.
    MEM (KB)  The memory it holds: its pages, its page tables and its
              kernel stack, as memstat counts them. A kernel thread
              shows -.

EXAMPLES
    top -d 0.5
    top -n 1 | cat
    top -n 1 -s mem | head -8
