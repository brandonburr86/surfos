tar - make, list and unpack archives

SYNOPSIS
    tar c [zv] [f archive] [-C dir] [--exclude=pattern] name...
    tar x [zv] [f archive] [-C dir] [--exclude=pattern] [name...]
    tar t [zv] [f archive] [--exclude=pattern] [name...]

DESCRIPTION
    c makes an archive of the names, with all that is in the folders
    among them. x unpacks one, t lists one. The letters may go together
    or apart, with a - before them or not: tar czf a.tgz dir, or
    tar -x -f a.tar.

    Without f, or with f -, the archive is standard input or output,
    so tar works in a pipe. It always reads forward. Archives from a
    Mac or Linux work: ustar, GNU long names and pax names and sizes.
    One made here is ustar, which they all read.

    With x and t, names choose the members to take; a folder's name
    takes what is in it, and case does not matter.

    SurfOS files have no owners or permissions, and tar cannot set a
    file's time: every file unpacked is as new. In an archive made
    here every file is 0644 and every folder 0755.

    tar refuses a name that is absolute or holds .., and one with a
    control character or a character FAT does not allow
    (: * ? " < > | \), or that ends in a space or a dot. Links, devices
    and sparse files are skipped.
    Each of those is said and the status is then 2; the entries around
    it are still taken. c leaves a leading / out of the names, and
    everything up to a .., and says so.

    A file that exists is replaced whole, by a new file beside it and
    a rename. A file that cannot be finished is deleted, or the old one
    stays. An archive that is cut short or damaged stops tar there, and
    what was unpacked before it stands. Being killed part way, by Ctrl+C
    or otherwise, is the exception: nothing can then tidy up, so a new
    file may stay short, and nothing marks it. Unpack again to be sure.
    A member that would land on the archive itself is skipped.

    With c, an archive that exists is replaced only if everything went
    in: otherwise the old one stays as it was, the status is 2, and
    that is said. The archive itself, found in a folder being archived,
    is left out with a note, which is no trouble. An archive that would
    hold nothing replaces nothing, and none is made. A name of more
    than 100 bytes without a / among its last 100 will not go into a
    ustar archive: it is left out, and said.

    The status is 0, or 2 if anything was skipped or went wrong.

OPTIONS
    c, x, t      What to do.
    z            The archive is compressed with gzip.
    v            Say each name: on standard error if the archive goes
                 to standard output. With t, also the mode, size and
                 date.
    f archive    The archive, - for standard input or output.
    -C dir       Go to dir first; for c, before the names after it.
    --exclude=pattern
                 Leave out what matches. * and ? are wildcards, and *
                 goes across a /. A pattern may match any run of whole
                 folders and names: tmp leaves out a/tmp/b. The case
                 of letters does not matter.

EXAMPLES
    tar czf /usb0p1/SITE.TGZ -C /hda1 www
    tar tzf /usb0p1/SITE.TGZ
    tar xzf /usb0p1/SITE.TGZ -C /hda1/RESTORE
    tar cf - --exclude='*.tmp' docs | gzip > /usb0p1/DOCS.TGZ
