fsck - check a FAT volume

SYNOPSIS
    fsck [-v] <device>

DESCRIPTION
    Checks a FAT12, FAT16 or FAT32 volume on a device lsblk lists,
    such as usb1p1: the boot sector and its backup, FSInfo, whether
    the two FATs agree, every directory and name, every chain of
    clusters and the size it holds, and the clusters nothing reaches.
    It only reads: nothing on the device changes.

    Repairs are not available in this version. fsck -r is refused,
    with status 2: "repairs are not available in this version, and
    the stick can be repaired on another computer". There: First Aid
    in Disk Utility on a Mac, chkdsk /f on Windows, fsck.fat on Linux.
    Copy off what you can first.

    fsck is a command of the kernel, because programs cannot read a
    disk. It takes the device, not a path. A volume mounted read-only
    is checked under its mount, which cannot go meanwhile. One mounted
    read-write is refused, because a volume in use changes under a
    check: umount it, or mount it with -r. A mount of its disk, or of
    a partition of it, is refused too.

    The network settings mount the stick SurfOS started from on
    /.netman for a moment while they are read or saved. fsck never
    holds that mount: it waits up to five seconds for it to go, and
    then says to try again. The other way round, while fsck looks at
    that stick unmounted, the settings cannot be saved: a save waits
    five seconds for it, and gives up.

    Some problems leave it unsure what the volume was meant to hold,
    and a repair anywhere has to guess at them: copy off what you can
    before one. These are such doubts:

      - a boot sector that differs from its backup in the volume's
        shape, or a FAT much larger than its clusters need, or holding
        something past its last entry, or wrong reserved FAT entries
      - FATs that are not mirrored
      - a first FAT and a second that disagree about a chain
      - a blank sector of the first FAT where the second has more, or
        a chain into a sector of the FAT that is all zeros
      - two names on one chain, of files or directories
      - a chain into a cluster marked bad, or a first cluster that is
        no cluster
      - a directory it cannot read, one more than 128 levels down, one
        of more than 65536 slots, one with no "." and ".." or a "."
        that names another cluster (it may be a damaged file), a
        directory whose chain has anything wrong with it, anything
        past a directory's end mark, a directory with no cluster, a
        label with clusters
      - a size of 0 on what looks like a directory
      - clusters nothing reaches, when they are most of what is in
        use, or when one of them starts as a directory

    What it could not check it says, and never calls a volume clean
    then: a block it cannot read, the directories below 128 levels,
    the slots of a directory past 65536, and the names of a directory
    with more of them than a check has memory to compare ("not
    checked whole").

    A check takes all it needs at its start, and makes the kernel's
    memory grow by 4096 KB at most: three bitmaps of one bit a
    cluster, room for the names of one directory (1.5 MB on any but a
    tiny volume) and buffers. A 118 GB stick with 32 KB clusters
    takes 3.3 MB. Past about 5.75 million clusters (188 GB with 32 KB
    clusters) less room is left for names, and a directory with more
    of them than it holds is not checked whole; a volume of more than
    about 9.93 million clusters (325 GB with 32 KB clusters, 41 GB
    with 4 KB) is refused, with what it would need: check it on
    another computer. Ctrl+C stops a check.

OPTIONS
    -v    Say every problem and the counts, not just the first 40.

EXIT STATUS
    0     Clean.
    1     Problems found.
    2     -r, a wrong command, a device it may not use, no FAT
          volume, a device that failed, no memory, a volume too large
          for a check, or a check that was not whole and found
          nothing else.
    130   Ctrl+C stopped it, as it stops any command.

EXAMPLES
    fsck usb1p1
    mount -r usb1p1
    fsck -v usb1p1

SEE ALSO
