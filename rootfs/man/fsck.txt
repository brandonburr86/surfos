fsck - check a FAT volume, and repair it on request

SYNOPSIS
    fsck [-v] <device>
    fsck -r [-v] <device>

DESCRIPTION
    Checks a FAT12, FAT16 or FAT32 volume on a device lsblk lists,
    such as usb1p1: the boot sector, FSInfo, whether the two FATs
    agree, every directory, every chain of clusters, and the clusters
    nothing reaches. Without -r it writes nothing.

    fsck is a command of the kernel, because programs cannot read a
    disk. It takes the device, not a path, and a volume mounted
    read-write is refused: umount it first, or mount it with -r. A
    mount of its disk, or of a partition of it, is refused too.

    With -r it says what it will change, on the console even when the
    output goes to a file, and asks for the device's name. Keys typed
    before the question are dropped. Only what mkfs may change can be
    repaired: a USB drive or a ramdisk, not mounted, and never the
    disk SurfOS started from.

    When in doubt, it changes nothing. A repair comes after a look at
    the whole volume, and any doubt in that look means nothing at all
    is written, FSInfo included: it says "no repair" and why. Copy off
    what you can, or look at it on another computer. These are doubts:

      - a boot sector that differs from its backup in the volume's
        shape, or a FAT much larger than its clusters need, or holding
        something past its last entry, or wrong reserved FAT entries
      - FATs that are not mirrored
      - a first FAT and a second that disagree about a chain: a link
        each its own, or the first ending a file or a directory
        early where the second goes on
      - a blank sector of the first FAT where the second has more, or
        a chain into a sector of the FAT that is all zeros
      - two names on one chain, of files or directories
      - a chain into a cluster marked bad, or a first cluster that is
        no cluster
      - a directory it cannot read, one more than 128 levels down, one
        with no "." and ".." or a "." that names another cluster (it
        may be a damaged file), a directory whose chain has anything
        wrong with it, anything past a directory's end mark, a
        directory with no cluster, a label with clusters
      - a size of 0 on what looks like a directory
      - clusters nothing reaches, when they are most of what is in
        use, or when one of them starts as a directory
      - a FAT12 entry across two sectors that would have to change

    Nothing is ever deleted, and when there is no doubt it repairs
    only these:

      - the second FAT, from the first
      - the end of a chain: a link to no cluster or back into its own
        chain ends where it stands, and a free cluster a chain runs
        into stays, as the end, because that cluster is read as part
        of the file
      - a size longer than its chain
      - clusters nothing reaches, saved as FOUND.000/FILE0000.CHK
        (their true size is not known: whole clusters are saved)
      - FSInfo's count, and last the mark that it was unmounted cleanly

    The rest it only reports, and repairs what it can around it: a
    chain longer than its size, ".." entries, long names, short names,
    a directory's size, FSInfo without its signatures, a lost chain
    that runs into another chain or a bad cluster, and the boot
    sector's own dirty flag. No repair changes an entry's first
    cluster. The mark of a clean unmount stays while any problem is
    left.

    A stick pulled out during a repair is left no worse, if two things
    hold: a sector is written whole, and the stick has its data safe
    when it says so. Many sticks promise that and do not keep it, and
    fsck cannot tell. The order of its writes then helps less, though
    it never writes over anything a name or a link reaches; what is
    left may need a person. Plug it in and run fsck -r again. Ctrl+C
    stops it between two steps.

OPTIONS
    -r    Repair, after asking.
    -v    Say every problem and the counts, not just the first 40.

EXIT STATUS
    0     Clean, or all of it repaired.
    1     Problems are left.
    2     A wrong command, a device it may not use, no FAT volume, or
          a device that failed.

EXAMPLES
    fsck usb1p1
    umount /usb1p1
    fsck -r usb1p1
