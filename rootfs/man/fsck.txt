fsck - check a FAT volume, and repair it on request

SYNOPSIS
    fsck [-v] <device>
    fsck -r [-v] <device>

DESCRIPTION
    Checks a FAT12, FAT16 or FAT32 volume on a device lsblk lists,
    such as usb1p1: the boot sector, FSInfo, whether the two FATs
    agree, every directory, every chain of clusters, and the clusters
    nothing reaches. Without -r it writes nothing.

    fsck is a command of the kernel, because programs cannot read a
    disk. It takes the device, not a path, and a volume mounted
    read-write is refused: umount it first, or mount it with -r.

    With -r it says what it will change and asks for the device's
    name. Only what mkfs may change can be repaired: a USB drive or a
    ramdisk, not mounted, and never the disk SurfOS started from.

    Nothing is ever deleted. It frees no cluster. It repairs these:

      - the second FAT, from a sound first one
      - a chain cut at its first bad link, the size set to match
      - a size longer than its chain
      - a chain longer than its size: what is cut off is saved
      - two names on one chain: the second gets its own copy,
        when there is room
      - clusters nothing reaches, saved as FOUND.000/FILE0000.CHK
        (their true size is not known: whole clusters are saved)
      - FSInfo, and last the mark that it was unmounted cleanly

    The rest it only reports: the boot sector, its backup, the "." and
    ".." entries, long names, short names, two names on one directory,
    and a repair of a FAT12 entry that spans two sectors. The mark of
    a clean unmount stays while any problem is left.

    It repairs nothing at all when it cannot trust what it saw: a
    directory that cannot be read, one whose chain runs into a free or
    bad cluster, one without its "." and ".." (it may be a damaged
    file), a size of 0 on what looks like a directory, two FATs that
    differ where the first has bad links the second does not, or FATs
    that are not mirrored. Copy off what you can, or look at it on
    another computer.

    A stick pulled out during a repair is left no worse. Plug it in
    and run fsck -r again. Ctrl+C stops it between two steps.

OPTIONS
    -r    Repair, after asking.
    -v    Say every problem and the counts, not just the first 40.

EXIT STATUS
    0     Clean, or all of it repaired.
    1     Problems are left.
    2     A wrong command, a device it may not use, no FAT volume, or
          a device that failed.

EXAMPLES
    fsck usb1p1
    umount /usb1p1
    fsck -r usb1p1
