wchat - Chat, an IRC client in a window

SYNOPSIS
    wchat

DESCRIPTION
    Talks on IRC: channels, private talks with one person (queries),
    nicks, topics. It needs a desktop: start it from Apps > Programs
    (Chat), its tile, the desktop's Terminal, or Apps > Run program.

    The window opens on a form: the server, the port, TLS (on, port
    6697; off, port 6667), the nick, and a password if the server or
    your account wants one: the server's own, or SASL's, which logs in
    to the account of the nick. The password shows as dots, goes to
    the server once and is wiped: nothing keeps it, so each connection
    asks for it again. Without TLS it crosses the network as it is.

    With TLS the server's certificate must be from a trusted issuer
    (the roots of /initrd/etc/ssl/cert.pem), for the server's name, and
    valid now, as Undertow checks a web server's. Nothing turns those
    checks off. TLS needs a CPU with RDRAND and a clock that is right.

    The rooms are tabs: the server's first, then the channels and the
    queries, each with how many lines came since you last looked at it,
    and a ! for one that names you. A channel shows its topic and its
    nick list (@ operators, + voices). Lines carry the time they came;
    the formatting codes others send (bold, colours, underline) are
    drawn, or left out by View > Formatting.

    Type a message and press Enter, or a command:

        /join #channel [key]   join it (and show its room)
        /part [#channel] [why] leave it (the room's own channel)
        /msg nick text         say text to nick, or to a channel
        /me does something     an action in this room
        /nick name             another nick
        /topic [text]          set the channel's topic, or ask it
        /query nick [text]     a room for talking with nick
        /quit [why]            leave the server

    //text sends text that starts with one /.

KEYS
    Enter                 send the line
    Up, Down              the lines sent before
    Page Up, Page Down    scroll the messages
    Ctrl+Page Up, Down    the room before, the room after
    Alt+1 to Alt+9        the rooms by their places
    Ctrl+W                close the room (leave its channel)
    Ctrl+N                connect (while not connected)
    Ctrl+Q                close Chat, saying goodbye first

LIMITS
    Chat does not do DCC, run scripts, open links, answer CTCP
    requests (it shows them), or keep anything: no logs, no passwords,
    no settings. 64 rooms, 2000 lines a room, 8 MB of lines in all;
    8192 nicks a channel. A line from the server over 8703 bytes is
    left out. What you send is paced, a line every two seconds after
    the first four, so a long paste is never thrown out for flooding.
    The rooms stay after a disconnection, for the same server, but
    the channels are not joined again by themselves. Only IRC's own
    character sets: UTF-8, or windows-1252 for text that is not.
