SurfCPU ddr track evidence: LiteDRAM's clock against nextpnr's timing, what was tried
Recorded 2026-10-16 (round 2) from the open flow (surfcpu-openxc7:2026-10-15: Yosys 0.69, nextpnr-
himbaechel 1.0.0) on surfcpu_arty_a7_ddr_bramdiag. The gated builds' own records are in
docs/evidence/board/openxc7-ddr.txt; the runs below are experiments, made with a scratch script
that is not kept (the same sources, defines and pin constraints as the design, a LiteDRAM core
generated from ip/litedram/arty.yml with one setting changed, tools/openxc7-build.sh --no-check),
recorded here for the reasoning.
Each line is nextpnr's "Max frequency" after routing for dram_clk, LiteDRAM's core clock (the
controller, the calibration CPU and its bus, the adapter and the bridge's controller side; its
constraint is the PLL's 100 MHz output, or 80 MHz where the core was asked for 80).

The DDR3L sets the floor: the MT41K128M16's DLL needs a clock period of at most 3.3 ns (at least
303 MHz); LiteDRAM's A7 PHY runs the memory at 4 x its core clock, so the core clock cannot go
below about 76 MHz.

Reading: the late paths are either inside LiteDRAM's controller (the ZQ-calibration timer
through the refresher and the command multiplexer to a bank machine's counters) or between the
calibration CPU and LiteX's bus inside the core; in both, routing is three quarters of the delay
(long hops of 1 to 1.7 ns between distant placements) and the logic is 13 to 17 LUTs deep as
Yosys maps it. No generator option touches either structure enough, and the clock cannot go
below about 76 MHz (the DDR3L's DLL). The flow's rule is that a missed estimate fails the build,
so the open flow gives the DDR designs no bitstream; nextpnr's delays are an estimate (OPEN-FLOW.md
6.4) and Vivado routinely closes LiteDRAM at 100 MHz on the Arty, which only a Vivado run (or
the board) can confirm.

What the numbers say:
  * Without the CPU the worst path is LiteDRAM's controller alone, every time: from the ZQ
    calibration timer (zqcs_timer_count0, a 27-bit down-counter compared with 0) through the
    refresher's state machine, the refresh request of the eight bank machines and the command
    multiplexer's arbitration into a bank machine's state (a synchronous reset or clock enable),
    15 to 19 LUT levels, 2.4 to 3.4 ns of logic and 7.8 to 10.4 ns of routing. At 100 MHz it
    needs 10 ns; 20 distinct placements reached 74.3 to 90.2 MHz.
  * LiteDRAM's settings move the structure little: command buffers of 8 or 4 entries, buffered,
    ZQ calibration at 128 Hz instead of 1 Hz, no auto-precharge: 79.8 to 87.5 MHz, inside the
    seeds' spread. cmd_latency is not used by the A7 PHY (the same netlist). The generated
    Verilog was not edited.
  * nextpnr's options alongside: rip-up gains 1 to 3 MHz on some placements and loses on others
    (76.3, 90.2, 79.0, 80.4), congestion-driven placement 86.6 (and 83.7 / 76.6 with rip-up), a
    higher timing weight loses (75.6), the annealing placer fails. None reaches 100 MHz; none is
    used.
  * 87.5 MHz (PLL VCO 1400: 200, 87.5 and 350 MHz) misses on both seeds (86.6, 84.6).
  * 80 MHz (VCO 1600: 200, 80, 320 at 0 and 90 degrees) closes on 9 of 12 placements (the 100T 5 of
    6, the 35T 4 of 6), the slowest 74.3 MHz. The DDR3L at 320 MHz (640 MT/s) is above its DLL's
    floor (tCK at most 3.3 ns: 303 MHz, a controller clock of 76 MHz); the generator's timings
    follow the clock and its mode registers stay as at 100 MHz (CL 6, CWL 5).
