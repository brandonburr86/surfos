    for a request to end when every buffer is busy, the bounce buffer's lock; the device drivers time out (USB mass
    storage 20 s a command, the SD card's and ATA's timeouts);
  a volume's lock: a task waiting for it holds nothing of the file system (it is counted only once it has the lock)
    and may be killed at once there;
  the task's own device request (blocks it marked read or written), a copy under way (its record, buffer and mark),
    and FAT's work under the volume's lock or in its mount and unmount (a chain freed, a directory walked): counted
    (fs_hold), the kill that waited comes when the task is out; the cache's lock and the bounce buffer's: their owner
    is never killed at once (bcache_holds()).
  A task ending by itself (TC_EXIT, its files closing) is never cut short; a hangup's kill (TI_HANGUP) cuts the waits
  short and is never escalated, as before.

  tests/host/test_mkfs.c (make unittest, devenv): MKFS 126 checks, 0 failures; the same test against 6ed8fcb's
    fs/fat_mkfs.c (built in the devenv, volume surfos-sc-fs10-base): 126 checks, 8 failures: "mkfs of a device it cannot
    take (busy) writes nothing and says why" and, for each of the 7 images, "every write made while mkfs holds the
    device, and let go of at its end"
  What is held, by whom and how long: cmd_mkfs() from the user's answer to its end (fat_mkfs(), whose own claim nests
    inside it, then set_type()'s part_commit(), whose claim of the disk nests too, or a whole disk's rescan);
    part_commit() from its second look at bdev_may_format() to the end of its rescan; fat_mkfs() called elsewhere (the
    self test, the host tests) from its check to its end. A claim of a partition keeps out requests to it and to its
    disk; a claim of a disk keeps out requests to it and to every partition of it; a sibling partition (another
    volume mounted on the same disk) is not affected
  The choice for the raw requests that keep arriving while it is held: refused at once (request_begin() returns
    BDEV_EBUSY, -6, in the same interrupts-off step as the look; the request never reaches its driver), not made to
    wait: waiting would need a wait queue and a wake-up at the claim's end, and would hold another task (a dump, a
    benchmark, a second fdisk) for a whole mkfs; refusing is what the busy rule does everywhere else (mount, mkfs,
    fdisk and sdmode refuse a busy device and say why). Who sees it: hexdump and readbench ("failed (-6: mkfs or fdisk
    is changing it)"), fdisk showing a held disk ("is being changed by mkfs or fdisk: try again once that has
    finished"), mount (the same words, before it reads), eject (refused in its first step), lsblk ("being changed").
    The cache sends none: a device held has nothing mounted, being mounted or being unmounted, so no volume's blocks
    are in flight to it; the USB hub thread's and the card watch's probes go to their drivers, not through the block
    layer, and are not refused
  Why the rescan can no longer be refused as busy: busy_why() for the disk at the rescan finds no mount (refused at the
    claim, and a mount after it refused), no unmount or eject (refused at the claim, an eject after it refused), no
    other task's claim (refused), and no request under way (one before the claim refused it; one after it refused at
    once; the claimant's own have ended). What remains: the disk unplugged (-ENODEV) or its block 0 unreadable (-1),
    and the commit or mkfs then fails saying so
  Cost: request_begin() looks at one global count (bdev_changes, 0 unless an mkfs or fdisk holds a device) before
    its increment, with interrupts off as before

== 67. B63: mutexes handed over in order
  The self test's step test_mutex_fifo (the test holds a lock of its own; another task queues for it; the test lets go
    and takes it again 50 times, never yielding in between, holding it 2 ms each time): "(the waiter had the lock at
    letting go number 1 of 50)": ok. 6ed8fcb: "number 51 of 50" (only after the loop): FAIL
  The hand-over (kernel/sync.c): mutex_unlock() with a waiter queued makes the first waiter the owner, counts the lock
    in its surf_task.mutexes and wakes it, all with interrupts off; mutex_lock() waits until it is the owner. Wait
    queues are FIFO (task_list_append(), wake_up() wakes the first). A mutex wait is not cut short (wait_prepare(),
    not _intr), so a waiter leaves the queue only when it is handed the lock or dies: kill_task_now() takes it off its
    list (become_zombie()) in an interrupts-off step, so either it is still queued and taken off before a hand-over
    can pick it, or it was handed the lock and counts it, and B62 then keeps the kill until it lets go. A wake-up for
    another reason (wake_up_task()) takes the waiter off the queue. This said it put the waiter at the end of the queue
    again, "a loss of place, never a lost lock": not so (round fs-fix-11, B66, section 73). When its holder had let go
    before it ran, mutex_unlock() had found nobody in line and left the mutex free, and the waiter went back in line
    and slept on the free mutex until another task took it and let go. Now it takes the free mutex. Nor does any such
    wake-up reach a mutex's waiter today, as this said fs/poll.c's and the pager's did: the timers of fs/poll.c and
    wait_on_timeout() are cancelled before their task goes on, and the pager's call-off wakes only its asker, which
    waits for a key or the console's lock, not a mutex
  Every mutex on both kernels, how it nests, and whether anything depended on taking it back at once (completed in
  round fs-fix-11, B67, section 74: seven nestings were missing, none reversing an order):
    mounts (mount_lock, fs/vfs.c): innermost: none of its regions takes another lock or waits (they look at the
      mounts, the devices' counts and the open files, with interrupts off at most); let go of around each volume's
      sync (vfs_sync()), nothing else held then. Taken alone, or under USB storage's m->lock and the hub thread's
      usb_lock (lun_probe() -> pick_name() -> vfs_disk_name_mounted(), driver/usb/msc.c: a new disk's name), under
      fdisk's write_lock (part_commit(): bdev_may_format() and vfs_bdev_change_begin(), its looks at the busy rule;
      bdev_rescan() -> vfs_bdev_busy()), and under a Wi-Fi station's hw_lock (iwl_start() reads its firmware file:
      vfs_stat() and vfs_open(), each a walk() through find_mount())
    a volume's (sb->lock, fat_lock()): outermost of the file system's; the cache's, the bounce buffer's and a driver's
      under it
    the cache's (bcache_lock): under a volume's or alone; never with the bounce buffer's; let go of and taken again
      around its sleeps and device requests while a volume's may be held: no waiter for it holds or wants a volume's
      (the cache never calls into a file system); nothing is taken under it. Also under the hub thread's usb_lock (a
      USB disk dropped: disk_drop() -> bdev_unregister() -> binvalidate(), in msc_service() with m->lock let go of
      first and in msc_detach()), under fdisk's write_lock (bdev_rescan() -> bdev_unregister() and binvalidate()),
      and under a Wi-Fi station's hw_lock (the firmware file's reads: /initrd is a tar file system read through the
      cache)
    the bounce buffer's (bounce_lock): under a volume's or alone; a driver's under it (the request)
    fdisk's (write_lock): mutex_trylock() only (another fdisk is refused), never waited for, so nothing can wait in
      a circle through it; its requests take a driver's, its looks at the busy rule mount_lock, its rescan mount_lock
      and bcache_lock
    USB storage's (m->lock): under the hub thread's usb_lock (attach, poll) or alone (requests); ep0's and the xHCI
      command lock under it, and mount_lock (a new disk's name, pick_name()); let go of and taken again around a new
      disk's partition scan in lun_probe() while the hub thread holds usb_lock: a request waiting for it takes no
      usb_lock; mutex_trylock() in msc_service() skips a busy device, as before
    usb (usb_lock): outermost of USB; the hub thread's poll and usb_print()/usb_snapshot()/the stall test; under it
      USB storage's m->lock, ep0's and the command lock, and net_lock (an adapter's unit, unit_for()), mount_lock (a
      new disk's name) and bcache_lock (a disk dropped)
    a USB device's ep0 (ep0_lock): under m->lock or usb_lock; the xHCI command lock under it
    the xHCI command lock (cmd_lock): innermost
    an ATA channel's (ch->lock): under the bounce buffer's or alone; nothing under it
    the SD card's (s->lock): under the bounce buffer's or alone (requests), or the card watch's; nothing under it
      (the watch names a new card's disk, and drops a card taken out, with s->lock let go of)
    net (net_lock): alone, or under dhcp_lock or ping_lock (held across their exchanges; nothing waiting for net_lock
      takes them), or under the hub thread's usb_lock (unit_for(), driver/usb/usbnet.c: an adapter taking a unit's
      name has the unit's addresses cleared under net_lock); no holder of net_lock takes usb_lock (a send through a
      USB adapter, usbnet_send(), takes no mutex: interrupts off around usb_poll() and usb_submit())
    dhcp (dhcp_lock): mutex_trylock() and a 50 ms sleep (one at a time): never queued, unchanged
    ping (ping_lock): outer of net_lock
    a Wi-Fi station's (hw_lock): outer of the iwl locks, and of mount_lock and bcache_lock while iwl_start() reads
      the firmware file (/initrd/lib/firmware/; with another file system mounted at /initrd, of what its reads take);
      nothing in the file system, the cache or the drivers takes it; mutex_trylock() in wlan_describe() says "busy",
      as before
    its scan list (list_lock): alone
    iwl commands (cmd_lock): outer of rx_lock, which iwl_cmd() lets go of and takes again around the command's wait
      while it holds cmd_lock: the waiter for rx_lock (iwl_poll() from the iwl thread) takes no cmd_lock under it
    iwl receive (rx_lock): under cmd_lock or alone; tx_lock under it (a transmit done); received frames go to the
      station layer through a queue (wlan_rx() copies them under irq_save()), so no lock of the station's is taken
      under it
    iwl transmit (tx_lock): innermost
    random (random_lock): alone. crypto_random() is called holding no lock: dns_random16() (net/dns.c:44) at line
      94, after dns_lookup() lets net_lock go at line 92, and at line 54 as udp_open()'s argument, worked out before
      udp_open() takes net_lock (net/udp.c:45); the supplicant's message 1 (net/wlan/wpa.c:371) from the station
      thread's rx_all(), holding none; the simulated access point's (driver/wifi/wlansim.c) from its thread, outside
      its net_lock section. (The review of B61-B65 counted it under net_lock: it is not)
    and the self tests' (selftest, rvtest) and crashlock
  No order is reversed anywhere (no lock is taken under one that it is ever held over), so the hand-over adds no
  deadlock; the only change is who goes next

== 73. B66: a mutex's waiter woken another way takes the free mutex
  The self test's step test_mutex_wake (both kernels: the test holds a mutex of its own; a thread queues for it; with
  interrupts off the test wakes the thread with wake_up_task() and lets the mutex go, before the thread runs):
    0062c33 (and 3dfd49b), x86 at -O0 (the transcripts) and at -O2 (the review's tree, section 72): "(the waiter had the
      mutex within 500 ms of the letting go)": ok; riscv32 (test_os.py, -O2 and -O0): ok
    d4050ed (the base harness, x86 -O0): "(the waiter did NOT have the mutex within 500 ms of the letting go; the mutex
      was free meanwhile, its waiter asleep in line)": FAIL; riscv32 -O2 (test-os-qemu): the same step FAIL, 134 of 135
      checks
    The review's R11b on this tree: section 72
  The change (kernel/sync.c, mutex_lock()): the wait loop looks at locked before each wait, and a free mutex is taken
  there (locked, owner, the task's count), as on arrival, which is as before. d4050ed's loop waited until the task was
  the owner, which only a hand-over makes it
  Why it is right:
    a free mutex has nobody in line: mutex_unlock() lets it go free only when its queue is empty, and a task finding it
      free takes it rather than joining the queue, all with interrupts off; so a task taking it free passes nobody
    the woken waiter is off the queue (wake_up_task() -> wake_locked() takes it off its list) when it looks: a later
      mutex_unlock() cannot hand it the mutex as well, so it is never counted twice and never owns it twice
    a waiter handed the mutex finds itself the owner and leaves the loop at once (counted by mutex_unlock()); one woken
      while the mutex is held by another (handed to a waiter ahead of it, or taken by an arriving task) queues again at
      the end: FIFO among those in line stands; the woken one loses its place, not the mutex
    the count: incremented once on each path (taken free on arrival or in the loop, handed over by mutex_unlock(),
      mutex_trylock()), decremented once by mutex_unlock(); a waiter killed while in line is taken off the queue by
      become_zombie() in an interrupts-off step before any hand-over can pick it, so a dead task is never handed a
      mutex (B62 keeps an owner from dying with one)
  Who calls wake_up_task() today: fs/poll.c's and wait_on_timeout()'s timers (cancelled under irq_save() right after
  their task's yield() returns, before it can go on to a mutex), the pager's call-off (console_page_call_off(): its
  asker waits for a key, tty_getc_unless(), or the console's lock, con_lock(), which loops on its own condition), and
  the self tests. None reaches a mutex's waiter: B66 was latent; the review made the wake-up itself (R11b)

== 83. B73: the waits under a held reference, and how long a killed waiter waits
  Where fs_refs is above 0, and every wait reachable from there (both kernels; riscv32 has no processes, sockets or

      the cache; vfs_sync() takes mount_lock again while it holds a root

      at a terminal, the pager's key and a pty's room)

      letting go): a last one's write-out (the volume's sync, bsync()) and its vnode's release
    reap(): the closes above, a socket's (tcp_release(): net_lock), a pty master's (master_release(): no wait), the

  Each wait, cut short by the kill or bounded by a driver:
    the cache's (a block under another task's request, a free buffer, the bounce buffer, its timed waits): cut short by
      the kill (fs_cut_short(), B39), but in a task ending by itself (TC_EXIT: its own closes, which no kill reaches)
      and in a step that must finish (B46: umount and eject's unmount, neither of which runs under such a reference:
      they take the mount's own root and no vnode through vhold())
    the pager's key: page_may_wait() refuses once the task's call is to end, and its wait is cut short; a pty's room for
      output and a terminal's read: cut short (wait_prepare_intr(), task_interrupted())
    the console's lock: held while a line is drawn, never across a wait for a key (the pager gives it back to wait)
    mount_lock: innermost, none of its regions waits (section 67)
    net_lock, in a reap's close of a socket: tcp_release() never waits, and every holder lets it go around its waits
      (net/tcp.c's WAIT_FOR)
    a device request: not cut short once sent (B47: counted inside), bounded by its driver: one of the cache's requests
      (64 KiB) within 583 s on USB storage with a healthy controller and 1,136 s at worst (B64, B71), 262 s on ATA,
      about 68 s for a write and 14 s for a read on the SD card, 2 minutes for a card without multi-block commands
      (section 57)
    a driver's lock (USB storage's m->lock, an ATA channel's, the SD card's): handed over in order (B63), each holder
      bounded the same way; USB storage's hub thread holds m->lock up to 148 s polling a unit with a disk and up to 36
      minutes probing one without (441 s and about 103 minutes at worst: sections 68, 78)
    the volume's lock (fat_lock()): not cut short. A killed waiter waits for the operations queued ahead of it (in
      order, B63), then does its own step, whose further pieces its kill cuts short (B39: a long read or write stops
      before its next piece). An operation holds the lock for its whole run: its pieces (up to 64 KiB straight, one
      device request, or a block through the cache, a request or a wait for another task's), its FAT and directory
      blocks through the cache, and for a sync the volume's dirty blocks, at most a device's share of the cache
      (BCACHE_HOLD, 256 blocks) written in runs. So the wait is the queued operations' requests, each within its
      driver's bound: behind another task's 1 MiB write (16 pieces) a fraction of a second on a working stick; on a USB
      stick that answers every command just inside its timeouts up to 16 x 583 s, about 2.6 hours, with a healthy
      controller and 16 x 1,136 s, about 5 hours, at worst; longer behind a longer write, which its caller's length
      bounds (a write past a file's end writes the zeros up to it first: at most the 4 GiB a FAT file can reach). A
      waiter whose call has more steps queues again for each (a walk takes the lock once per component); the cache's
      waits inside them are cut short, which ends most of them at their first miss
  None is unbounded and uncut: a kill that waited comes within these bounds, and with working devices within a few ticks
  of the call letting go (section 82: within the tick). Before B73 the same tasks were killed at once (a kernel thread
  at its kill, a call KILL_WAIT_MS after it) and left the references behind. Not done: making a walk stop between its

  clean-up fail (fsbench removes its file "whatever ends it" through a walk; eject's write-out opens each mount)
  (Round fs-fix-12, B76, section 90: the kill that waited came at once at the first look after the task let go, where

  on. B79, section 93: a task that keeps making VFS calls is killed within about 2 x KILL_WAIT_MS)

== 100. The kill rule re-derived (section 90, with B80-B82's marks): what it guarantees and what it does not
  Marks (surf_task.intr): TI_KILL, a kill; TI_NOCALL, killed outside any call (looks every FS_HOLD_RETRY_MS); TI_HELD,
  a grace owed; TI_GRACE, the grace begun (once); TI_LATE, the grace over with the task holding something; TI_FSWAIT,
  the log's line said; TI_HANGUP, a hangup's kill (in a call it waits for the call's end, one line, no look after). All
  but TI_INT come with TI_KILL and go with the call's end (intr = 0) or the task's death
  The kill comes at the end of the task's call (task_call_leave(): task_exit(-1) in its own context, its files
  closed), or a kernel thread ends by itself; at once (become_zombie()) only (1) at kill_task() or kill_hangup(), to a
  task outside any call holding nothing (2004's rule), (2) at a call's KILL_WAIT_MS look (B48's too), to a call holding
  nothing and not marked TI_HELD (2004's rule), (3) at a look with TI_GRACE, to a task holding nothing. Never while the
  task holds something (in_fs(): fs_hold with fs_finish, the cache's lock, a mutex but the console's, fs_refs): every
  look that finds it so marks it and looks again FS_HOLD_RETRY_MS later. (A fault is no kill: kill_task_now(), B56.)
  TI_HELD is set: by kill_task() and kill_hangup() finding it holding; by a look finding it holding; by B48's hand-over
  (kept, and set when holding then); by task_vfs_begin_slow() at the start of every VFS call a killed task makes but a
  hangup's in a call or a task ending
  The grace begins (TI_HELD, no TI_GRACE, holding nothing): at a look; or at the start of a VFS call when TI_HELD was
  set before that call. It ends KILL_WAIT_MS later, at the look of the one timer it starts again (kill_task(),
  kill_hangup(), kill_escalate() and task_vfs_begin_slow() start that timer; task_exit() and become_zombie() cancel
  it; no look comes during a grace, and a VFS call begun in it finds TI_GRACE set). At its end: holding nothing, the
  task is killed at once (3), the log saying "did not end ... within 2000 ms of letting go: killed at once"; holding
  something, TI_LATE: from then every VFS call but a close says EINTR before it takes anything (in a step that must
  finish none), the log saying once "... again ..., its 2000 ms from letting go over: killed as soon as it lets go"
  (B82), and the first look that finds it holding nothing kills it (3). B48's hand-over (a thread outside any call that
  entered one) starts the call's rule afresh, its grace included: two graces at most for such a thread
  So, by kind of task:
    a kernel thread outside any call: killed at once if it holds nothing at the kill; otherwise marked and looked at
      every 20 ms; its grace begins at the first look or VFS call that finds it holding nothing (R13a: before, a look
      that had not seen it holding killed it at once); it ends by itself in the grace, or is killed at its end
      holding nothing, or after it (TI_LATE)
    a call (a system call, a kernel command, a program being loaded): marked if it holds something at its kill or
      begins a VFS call after it; its KILL_WAIT_MS look finds it holding (marked, looked at every 20 ms, its grace at
      the letting go), or out and marked (its grace from that look: R13b), or out and not marked (killed at once)
    a hangup's kill: in a call it waits for the call's end (B55); outside any call (a task holding something: none
      can be, a process outside any call holds nothing) as a kill
    a task in a step that must finish (fs_finish: umount, eject's unmount): held, so never killed at once there; never
      refused
    a task ending (task_exit(): TC_EXIT): no kill reaches it
  Guaranteed: a task whose kill found it holding something, or that has taken or begun to take something since (a look
  saw it holding, or it began a VFS call), is never killed at once before KILL_WAIT_MS after the first moment it was
  seen holding nothing; one that ends in that time ends with its clean-up done, at its call's end (status -1) or by
  itself (its own status). A task that ignores its kill and works on through VFS calls is killed within about
  KILL_WAIT_MS of its first letting go plus the operation then under way (R12b's longest at the code final: 4630 ms at
  -O0, 4670 ms at -O2). The count of what a call holds (B73's fs_refs, B74's spawn, B75's reap) is let go of on every
  way out
  Not guaranteed: (a) the grace is once: a task holding something again when its grace ends is killed at the first
  look after its next letting go, its clean-up after that cut short; from then its VFS calls are refused so that this
  comes soon. (b) A call never marked (holding nothing at its kill, no VFS call since, holding nothing at its
  KILL_WAIT_MS look) is killed at once at that look wherever it is, as in 2004: a lock taken and let go between the
  kill and that look outside any VFS call (a network call's net_lock) is not seen; marking at every mutex_lock() would
  cost every lock a load (B66's first version showed what that costs on SurfCPU). (c) A task that never lets go (a lock
  or a file kept while it works) is not killed while it holds (B39, B62, B73): its waits there are cut short or end
  within its drivers' bounds (section 83), and a command looks for its kill between its steps; a loop that holds
  something throughout and never looks is not killed. (d) A hangup's kill in a call waits for the call's end, however
  long (B55). (e) A loop that takes a lock outside any VFS call each time round (none does today) is killed only when a
  look finds it out, as before B79
  Every count's drop of section 90 is followed now by the grace whichever way the task was found holding: before, a
  thread whose kill kill_task() itself put off, or a call killed holding something and out by its KILL_WAIT_MS look,
  met the kill at its first look after the drop (B80); what follows each drop is unchanged (section 90's list), and so
  is its conclusion: it ends the call or the thread within milliseconds, waiting only where the kill cuts the wait
  short or on a bounded holder
  The review's counters (r13_patch.py, section 96) at the code final: -O0 "(put-off thread kills landing at a look with
  no grace 0; calls killed at once at KILL_WAIT_MS with no grace 0; graces begun by a look 11, by a path call 92;
  TI_LATE set 82, EINTR returned 406321; killed at once after a grace: threads 25, calls 63; TI_* bits without TI_KILL
  0)", -O2 the same but for its counts (by a path call 86, TI_LATE 75, after a grace 26 and 57). "By a path call" counts
  a grace begun at the start of any VFS call now (B81)
